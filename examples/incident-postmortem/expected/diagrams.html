Eight figures, read in order. The first two are the cause: a change that reached further than it was meant to, and a number small enough to look harmless in daylight. The next two are why it was misread and why it escaped the building: every health signal read green while the workers starved, and one message, restarted mid-flight, became a second email because nothing downstream checks whether it has already been sent. The fifth and sixth put that whole mechanism on the night's real clock, the seventh divides the clock into the two waits and the two decisions that bracket them, and the last asks how much of that clock is actually evidence. One pen per person throughout: Nadia Brandt, who was paged and restarted the fleet, and Colm Ferreira, who shipped the change and found it; system behaviour that neither of them chose sits in ink-soft.

Reaching the ingest fleet was only half of it. Four connections had to also be a number small enough to look harmless and still be fatal.

A starved worker is still a running worker, and that is exactly what the dashboard said it was.

Restarting a starved worker is survivable. Restarting one that is holding an unacknowledged message is what reached a customer.

That is one message. Widen it to the whole night and the same restart sits in the middle of a chain that started somewhere else entirely.

That chain accounts for the failure. It says nothing about how long each link took, which is where the night was actually spent.

Two of those eight points are decisions a person made. The other stretches are waiting, and the split between them is not even.

Every one of those boundaries is a timestamp, and they are not all worth the same. Four came off a log; the rest came off a chart or out of a memory.
